# Luna hold recovery: four historical endorsement leads

**Research date:** 2026-08-22  
**Scope:** targeted archival recovery of four previously unresolved Toronto endorsement leads. This memo does not change the approved endorser panel or import any fact.

## Evidence rule

The project requires an explicit positive choice by the exact canonical endorser, tied to the canonical contest and candidate, with a source that is either first-party/publisher-owned or a reputable contemporaneous direct attribution. A search result, Wikipedia biography, bibliographic citation, or a later summary is a discovery lead only. The absence of a recovered source is not a non-endorsement.

## 1. Toronto Star → Adam Giambrone, 2003 Ward 18 — HOLD

The target is real: the 2003 Toronto municipal election included Giambrone in Ward 18, Davenport, and the City's election-results index is the appropriate official contest reference ([City election-results index](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/elections/election-results-reports/)). A bibliographic trail identifies the Star's November 6, 2003 Ward 18 article as “Slate of six compete for Ward 18” ([secondary bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Bail%C3%A3o)).

The only positive endorsement statement recovered in this pass is the sentence in Adam Giambrone's Wikipedia biography that says he had the Toronto Star's endorsement, alongside Now Magazine, the Bloor West Villager newspapers, and individual councillors ([biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Giambrone)). The page has no citation attached to that sentence, and the text of the Star article was not recovered. The contemporaneous UrbanToronto election-results thread instead attributes the Ward 18 choice it discusses to the **Labour Council**, not the Star ([thread](https://urbantoronto.ca/forum/threads/2003-municipal-election-toronto-council-results.385/)). That is not a Star bridge.

The Toronto Public Library confirms that its Toronto Star Historical Newspaper Archive contains complete scanned issues, including the November 2003-present run, and supports browsing a specific issue/page ([TPL archive guide](https://blogs.tpl.ca/database-guides/2020/10/getting-started-with-toronto-star-historical-newspaper-archives/)). That is a viable next retrieval route, but it is not an accessible evidence capture in this pass.

**Recommendation:** retain the candidate and article as a recovery lead; do not import a Star → Giambrone fact.

## 2. Toronto Star → David Miller, 2006 mayor — HOLD

The surviving bibliographic record identifies the item precisely as “Qualified support for David Miller,” an editorial in the *Toronto Star*, November 10, 2006, page A26 ([bibliographic record](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/5061563/)). The same record describes Miller as receiving a qualified Star endorsement shortly before election day, but it reproduces neither the editorial nor its actual choice language.

The existing project recovery report also identifies the official 2006 election report for contest validation ([City 2006 election report](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/8f92-2006-election-report.pdf)) and the Star item as a retrieval target ([newspaper archive recovery](endorsement-newspaper-archive-recovery.md#toronto-star-2006-mayor--david-miller)). No Star page, library scan, or reputable contemporaneous direct quotation was recovered here. The Toronto Public Library guide confirms that a complete scanned Star archive covering this issue exists for cardholders ([TPL archive guide](https://blogs.tpl.ca/database-guides/2020/10/getting-started-with-toronto-star-historical-newspaper-archives/)).

**Recommendation:** keep on hold. The title is a strong lead, not sufficient evidence by itself, particularly because “qualified support” may contain limitations that matter to the project’s endorsement semantics.

## 3. CUPE Ontario → David Miller, 2003 mayor — exact entity remains unresolved

The discovery trail points to James Cowan's *National Post* item “Union backs Miller for mayoralty run,” dated March 5, 2003, page A15. The trail is preserved in the David Miller biography and related bibliographic record ([Miller biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Miller_%28Canadian_politician%29); [bibliographic record](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/5061563/)). The available text says only that Miller received support from “the Canadian Union of Public Employees”/“CUPE.” It does not identify the CUPE Ontario division, a Toronto district council, a local, or the national union as the decision-maker.

The exact CUPE Ontario archive was checked for contemporaneous Toronto political material. Its July 14, 2006 article by Sid Ryan says CUPE Ontario would be active in the mayoral election and attacks Jane Pitfield's anti-union position, but it does not positively select Miller or any other candidate ([CUPE Ontario, “Gloves off in Jane's mayoral campaign”](https://cupe.on.ca/d307/gloves-off-jane-mayoral)). CUPE Ontario's later pages identify the division as the provincial political wing, but that organizational description cannot retroactively identify the body behind an unqualified 2003 “CUPE” reference ([CUPE Ontario, About Us](https://cupe.on.ca/about-us/about-cupe-ontario/)).

**Recommendation:** keep unresolved. Do not convert generic CUPE or labour support into the approved exact entity **CUPE Ontario**. A scan of the March 5, 2003 *National Post* item or a contemporaneous CUPE Ontario release naming Miller would be needed.

## 4. CUPE Ontario → Neethan Shan, 2025 Ward 25 — explicit fact, missing page date, panel excluded

CUPE Ontario's own page is explicit: “CUPE Ontario is proud to endorse Neethan Shan for Toronto City Council in the Ward 25 Scarborough by-election,” followed by “Vote for Neethan Shan.” ([CUPE Ontario, “Shan for Scarborough”](https://cupe.on.ca/shanforscarborough/)). This is exact-entity, first-party, positive endorsement language, and it maps to the Ward 25 candidate in the project's target contest. The page's visible text contains no publication date and says “Monday, September 30”; the by-election was held on Monday, September 29, 2025, so that line is not a reliable publication date.

A contemporaneous, dated source establishes pre-poll CUPE participation in the same campaign. The Toronto & York Region Labour Council's September 22, 2025 page explicitly endorses Shan for the September 29 by-election and advertises a September 23 canvass “with CUPE ON” ([Labour Council, September 22, 2025](https://www.labourcouncil.ca/elect-neethan-shan-in-scarborough-rouge-park/)). The underlying Action Network form independently preserves the September 14 and September 23 options “with CUPE Ontario” and identifies the Labour Council as sponsor ([Action Network form](https://actionnetwork.org/forms/labour-canvass-for-neethan-shan-in-scarborough-rouge-park)). These sources strongly corroborate contemporaneous CUPE Ontario involvement before polls closed, but they do not expose a publication timestamp for the CUPE Ontario endorsement page itself.

The official City financial-filing page validates the Ward 25 campaign universe and lists both Neethan Shan and Canadian Union of Public Employees (CUPE) Ontario among the filed campaign/third-party records ([City filing status](https://secure.toronto.ca/EFD/jsf/candidate2018/candidate_campaign_status.xhtml?campaign=23)). The City record is administrative validation, not proof of endorsement.

**Recommendation:**

- **Evidence status:** the endorsement statement itself is **confirmable** if the project accepts the dated Sept. 22/23 contemporaneous corroboration as the pre-poll date gate. If the gate requires the exact CUPE page or an archive capture to carry its own date, retain **HOLD** until that capture is recovered.
- **Dataset status:** do not import under the current panel configuration because CUPE Ontario is presently **not applicable to council contests**. This scope decision is independent of the quality of the recovered endorsement evidence.
