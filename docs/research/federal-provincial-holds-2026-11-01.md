# Federal and provincial identity holds (2026-11-01)

This pass took the 343 House of Commons and Ontario legislature Candidacies from 2004 to 2026 that
were still unresolved: 190 federal and 153 provincial. Each had a proposed Person from the
rule-based review, which held it because the name matched but the office, riding or party did not
line up well enough to link on results alone. None had been researched before.

Research agents searched public sources for each row: Wikipedia biographies and party candidate
lists, party and campaign pages, VoteMate and other candidate guides, and news and community
papers. The standard is the one used throughout the dataset. A source has to name both runs, or
describe the person with a distinctive fact (occupation, employer, organization, office held) that
matches across them. Same-office continuity is enough only when the riding, party and a short gap
line up under an uncommon name with no competing candidate, and a link across offices always needs
a person-specific source. The agents did not use LinkedIn, ProQuest or pages behind a login, and
the Canadian Elections Database, which groups candidates by name, was treated as a lead only.

Every proposed confirmation and join then went to a second agent that had not done the research.
It opened each cited page, checked each quote against the page and the record (riding, year, party, placing
and votes), and applied the same standard. Proposals that rested on the Canadian Elections Database
alone, or on structural continuity under a common name, were held before that check. Dispositions
are under reviewer `federal-provincial-holds-2026-11-01`.

Of the 343 rows, 110 are confirmed to their targets. The same sources confirm one run outside the
scope, Knia Singh's 1997 Ward 18 council run (printed as George B. Singh), and join 19 runs held by
15 other Person records to the Person they belong to. The other 233 rows remain unresolved.

Three confirmations are structural. Neil Spiegel's 2004 Parkdale—High Park run continues his 2000
run there. The earlier proposal named the Person holding his 2003 provincial run, a different
office, so the curated assertion `neil-spiegel-2004-house-continuity` joins the 2004 run to the
Person holding the 2000 run. Elections Canada's 2000 candidate file and 2004 results table both give
Neil Spiegel as the Green candidate in Parkdale—High Park. Sam Apelbaum's 2007 and 2011
Scarborough—Guildwood runs continue his Libertarian runs in Scarborough East, which
Scarborough—Guildwood partly replaced. Two Person records held those earlier runs until his
archived Wikipedia biography, which names all three, joined them.

Two confirmations and one join rest on archived copies of Wikipedia biographies that Wikipedia no
longer carries: Sheila White's, Rob Newman's and Sam Apelbaum's. Each copy gives the runs with the
official vote counts. The second check of Rob Newman's run was told not to use the archived copy and
found no other source, so the copy was read separately against the record.

  27 candidate, and it names no riding.
