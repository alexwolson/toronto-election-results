# Trustee and provincial candidate splits (2026-10-29)

Scope: three long-serving Toronto District School Board trustees who were each held as two Persons,
a trustee record and a same-name Ontario provincial candidate in the area the trustee represented.
The 2000 trustee review (`pre2003-trustee-2000-identity-review-2026-10-29.md`) left them as probable
splits without a source joining the two records. A registry scan for the same pattern turned up one
more pair that could be joined (Christopher Caldwell) and several that could not.

Standard: a link across office types needs a person-specific source that places the person in the
contest or states a career that covers it. Exact name is never enough. The pre-2003 municipal review
already accepted an Election Prediction Project riding page as such a source: Agnes Potts
(`can_5ed821fe5ab0592cbedd663504d4accc`) was confirmed on a single contributor comment, "former
Etobicoke councillor and school trustee Agnes Potts", on the 1999 Etobicoke Centre page. All three
bridges below come from pages on the same site.

## Irene Atkinson: merged

The Election Prediction Project's 1999 Parkdale—High Park page
(https://electionprediction.org/1999_ontario/toronto/parkdale-high-park.html) lists Irene Atkinson as
the NDP candidate and reproduces campaign coverage and reader comments:

- NOW Magazine, April 8, 1999, "Cooking up a Tory defeat" by Enzo di Matteo: "About the only Tory who
  stands a chance here is a former one who's since turned NDP candidate -- school trustee Irene
  Atkinson, who enjoys a strong local following and community credentials."
- NOW Magazine, March 11-17, 1999, "Tories creeping closer to big vote" by Enzo DiMatteo and Glenn
  Wheeler: "in Parkdale-High Park they couldn't choose between golden Grit MPP Gerard Kennedy and
  exTory Irene Atkinson, whose running for the NDP." (spelling as printed)
- Contributor "A.", March 14, 1999: "With a very strong candidate in trustee and former school board
  chair Irene Atkinson (who has support not only among the riding's traditional NDP base but also
  among Red Tories), the NDP is poised to slay a giant here."

The trustee record fits each of those descriptions:

- A Toronto Star photo caption of November 27, 1980 (Toronto Public Library, TSPA_0029955F,
  https://digitalarchive.tpl.ca/objects/240831/irene-atkinson-toronto-board-of-education-chairman-is-seen):
  "Irene Atkinson: Toronto Board of Education chairman is seen as a safe conservative likely to
  support Mayor Art Eggleton".
- Cheri DiNovo, MPP for Parkdale—High Park, in the Legislature on November 17, 2014
  (https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/house-documents/parliament-41/session-1/2014-11-17/hansard):
  "That's Irene Atkinson, our trustee, who is retiring after 40 years as a trustee" and "A true woman
  of conscience, she used to be a red Tory and left the Conservative Party after she saw what Mike
  Harris did to education in this province. She crossed the floor to us".
- Kristin Rushowy's Toronto Star obituary of July 31, 2021
  (https://torontostar.pressreader.com/article/281784222133780): "She morphed from a Progressive
  Conservative to a member of the NDP thanks to (former Conservative premier) Mike Harris."
- The Parkdale—High Park NDP (https://www.phpndp.ca/about): "In the past, we've elected strong New
  Democrat representatives at all levels including ... TDSB Trustees Irene Atkinson, Robin Pilkey,
  and Debbie King".

The 1999 coverage names the riding's NDP candidate as a school trustee and former Tory, and a
contributor at the time calls her a former school board chair. The Ward 7 trustee represented
Parkdale—High Park, chaired the Toronto Board of Education, and left the Conservatives for the NDP
during the Harris government. The NOW articles were read as excerpts on the prediction site; the
originals were not found online.

Sources checked that do not bridge the two records: Wikipedia's 1999 NDP candidate list (name only),
the Gerard Kennedy, Annamarie Castrilli and Cheri DiNovo articles, Hansard for 2009-09-30,
2010-03-02, 2010-04-21 and 2013-03-18 (statements about her as a trustee), Peggy Nash's 2013 Commons
statement, CP24 and Zoomer Radio on the 2013 fire, Bayview News (2014) and the 2021 obituary on Yahoo
News.

## Stephnie Payne: merged

The Election Prediction Project's 2003 York West page
(https://electionprediction.org/2003_on/riding/york-west.htm) has a comment by "A.S." dated 25/02/03:
"school trustee Stephnie Payne couldn't even earn back her deposit in falling to third in '99". The
page's 1999 result line reads "STEPHNIE PAYNE 3377 12.99%".

Elections Ontario's 1999 results give STEPHNIE PAYNE, New Democratic Party, York West, 3,377 votes,
third of seven (https://results.elections.on.ca/api/report-groups/6/report-outputs/532/csv).

On the trustee side, the Black Creek local-history interview
(https://sites.google.com/site/blackcreeklocalhistory/transcripts/stephnie-payne) introduces her as
"Public School Trustee for Ward 4 for the Toronto District School Board", and she says "I first got
elected as a trustee in 1991 and I ran in 1988 and was not successful" and "I ran again in 1991 and
won and have served since then."

The comment names the 1999 York West third-place finisher as school trustee Stephnie Payne, and the
official result agrees on the finish. Both records use the uncommon spelling Stephnie, and her
trustee ward covers York West. The deposit remark is the commenter's; her official share was 12.99%.

Sources checked that do not bridge the two records: Wikipedia's 1999 NDP list (it spells the name
"Stephanie" and carries no entry text; a redirect named Stephnie_Payne points to it), the 100ABCWomen
profile ("Stephnie Yarde-Payne", no party or election named), the Downsview Advocate (2014),
Greenwin's 2012 retirement post, a 2006 Ontario justice committee transcript and 2006 Hansard. A
search of Share's site found nothing.

## Bruce Davis: not merged

The Election Prediction Project's 2003 Etobicoke-Lakeshore page
(https://www.electionprediction.org/2003_on/riding/etobicoke-lakeshore.htm) has a comment by Adam
Sobolak dated 12/11/02. Dates on the 2003 pages are day first (the York West comment is 25/02/03), so
this is November 12, 2002: "Wasn't school trustee/former candidate/long-time Liberal operator Bruce
Davis vetted for the E-L Grit nomination?"

Supporting sources:

- Xtra, April 18, 2010, by Allison Martell
  (https://xtramagazine.com/power/smitherman-replaces-conservative-campaign-manager-with-liberal-35081):
  "Now Bruce Davis, a veteran Liberal organizer and school board trustee, is stepping in as campaign
  manager." and "Davis was first elected trustee in 2000, in the midst of amalgamation."
- The City of Toronto public appointee biography (https://secure.toronto.ca/pa/member/7764.do): "and
  for ten years served as a trustee for the Toronto District School Board." and "Bruce and his family
  have lived in Etobicoke's Long Branch neighbourhood for almost forty years."
- Elections Ontario's 1995 results: Bruce Davis, Liberal, Etobicoke-Lakeshore, 9,074 votes, second
  (https://results.elections.on.ca/api/report-groups/7/report-outputs/876/csv).

The comment ties the sitting trustee to an earlier candidacy and to Liberal organizing in this
riding, and Xtra confirms he was a Liberal organizer first elected trustee in 2000. Apart from that
2000 win, the only Bruce Davis candidacy in the dataset before the comment is the 1995 Liberal run in
Etobicoke-Lakeshore. Long Branch is in that riding, and he has lived there since the mid-1980s.

The gap is that no source names the 1995 contest. The dataset has no 1994 or 1997 school-board
results, so an unsuccessful trustee run in those years cannot be ruled out as the "former candidate"
the comment means.

The researcher proposed a merge assertion on this evidence; the primary reviewer did not accept it. The
only bridge is an unsourced comment whose "former candidate" does not name a contest, the name is
common, and an earlier unsuccessful trustee run is possible, so the 1995 candidacy stays on its own
Person (`per_2550e04d307f56558ceba2a6c7f2df26`). A source that names the 1995 contest would settle it.

Sources checked that do not bridge the two records: the Wikipedia articles on Etobicoke—Lakeshore and
Morley Kells (name only), Wikipedia's 1995 Liberal candidates page (no entry for him), the Globe and
Mail on his 2010 appointment ("a veteran of local politics"), Toronto Newswire (2025) and the ONPHA
conference speaker page.

## Christopher Caldwell: joined (found in the scan)

Christopher Caldwell's 2018 TDSB Ward 3 candidacy was its own Person. The 2018 Green provincial run
and the 2019 Green federal run in Etobicoke—Lakeshore were unresolved claims to
`per_4cdca864f8055243963b86909ed39038`, the Chris Caldwell who ran for council in Ward 29 in 2010.

The local news site Skedline profiled the Ward 3 trustee candidates in "Trustee candidates fight for
better schools in ward 3" by Liam Harrington and Justin Field (https://skedline.com/?p=18464).
The article is undated. It names Pamela Gough as the departing Ward 3 trustee and names Christopher
Caldwell, Aziza Mohammed and David Kalbfleisch, who are all on the 2018 Ward 3 ballot. It says "He ran
for the Green Party during this year's provincial election, and he ran for council a few years ago."
and "He is an Etobicoke local and has been for the majority of his life."

Elections Ontario's 2018 candidate file covers all 124 districts and has one Caldwell: Chris
Caldwell, Green Party of Ontario, Etobicoke—Lakeshore, 2,138 votes
(https://results.elections.on.ca/api/report-groups/1/report-outputs/932/csv). The assertion joins
that candidacy to the trustee Person. It does not join:

- The 2019 federal Green run in Etobicoke—Lakeshore (`can_98f13c94ab1f560994954198b31935dd`).
  Elections Canada's 2019 Table 12 gives residence Etobicoke and occupation "Learning Consultant",
  which fits Skedline's description of degrees in sustainable infrastructure and education but does
  not establish identity. It stays unresolved against `per_4cdca864f8055243963b86909ed39038`.
- The 2010 Ward 29 council run (`can_656ab05636855a8f856c3d8cde842334`). It is the only Toronto
  council run under the name, but the article does not say which council race he ran in. It stays
  on its own Person.
