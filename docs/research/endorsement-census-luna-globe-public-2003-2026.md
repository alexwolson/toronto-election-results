# Luna public discovery: Globe and Mail Editorial Board Toronto municipal endorsements, 2003–2026

**Research date/cutoff:** 2026-08-28
**Researcher:** Luna
**Endorser:** The Globe and Mail Editorial Board (`globe_and_mail_editorial_board`)
**Scope:** Toronto mayoral and City Council contests in the Results event set, covering the 2003–2026 general elections and Toronto council or mayoral by-elections in that period. Trustee-only events are excluded. This is a public discovery pass: no institutional login was used and no canonical curation, panel, or release output was changed.

## Executive findings

The public record provides three useful mayoral package leads:

1. **2003 general — John Tory.** A public reproduction of the Globe editorial *Choosing the next mayor of Toronto*, updated November 1, 2003, concludes with the board choosing Tory. The reproduction is a strong source-package lead, but the original Globe URL and page image still need archival validation.
2. **2010 general — George Smitherman.** Multiple contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous secondary sources attribute the Globe choice to Smitherman. The identified editorial is *The Globe's endorsement for mayor of Toronto* (the old Globe URL contains `article1767954`); the exact publication day is reported as October 21 or October 22, 2010 in the public record and should be confirmed against the newspaper/archive copy.
3. **2014 general — John Tory.** This is explicitly confirmed by the Globe public editor and by contemporaneous reporting. The exact editorial title and publication date are not yet recovered; one account says Friday, October 17, while another says Saturday, October 18. The article's quoted rationale is consistent with an editorial-board choice, not a columnist's view.

No complete Globe council slate was recovered publicly for any election. No qualifying Globe editorial-board package was recovered publicly for the 2006, 2018, or 2022 mayoral contests, or for the 2026 cycle as of the cutoff. No council or mayoral by-election package was recovered publicly. These are bounded retrieval gaps, **not inferred non-endorsements**.

## Method and evidence standard

I first checked the repository's existing endorsement coverage and research reports, then searched public Globe indexes, search-engine results, public reproductions, election references, media coverage, and archival citations. Searches used combinations of:

- `Globe and Mail`, `Globe editorial board`, `endorsement`, `choice`, and `editorial`;
- the election year and Toronto mayor or council;
- ward number and `by-election`; and
- recovered titles, old Globe URL slugs, and candidate names.

An item is treated as a positive editorial-board lead only where the source identifies the board/newspaper choice or reproduces the editorial itself. A Globe news story, municipal columnist, campaign claim, or a different organization's endorsement is not a Globe Editorial Board endorsement. Where the public record does not yield a package, the report records a search target rather than a negative.

## Board endorsement versus other Globe material

- The 2014 public editor explains that the Globe's editorial board speaks for the newspaper, while the newsroom and individual writers have distinct roles. This is why Marcus Gee's favourable discussion of Olivia Chow is not a competing board choice.
- Globe news stories about candidates, polls, campaign events, or endorsements by other people and organizations are not board endorsements.
- A post-election or policy editorial is not an election endorsement merely because it discusses a mayor or candidate. For example, the 2023 search surfaced a later editorial described by a personal blog as *Get the party started in Toronto*; it is post-election commentary, not evidence of a pre-election choice.
- Secondary sources are used here to identify and prioritize packages. They should not replace the original editorial where the source is available through an archive.

## Operational disposition

- Do not add Globe endorsement assertions from this report alone.
- Preserve the three mayoral leads as prioritized recovery targets: Tory (2003), Smitherman (2010), and Tory (2014). The 2014 choice has the strongest public confirmation; the other two require original-source validation.
- Treat all unresolved general-election council packages and all by-election searches as open retrieval gaps, not no-endorsement findings.
- When authenticated retrieval succeeds, record the original title, publication date, page/section, provider/database, retrieval date, and a concise evidence summary, then resolve each named candidate to the canonical contest and candidacy IDs.
- If no package is found after a documented archive search, retain the bounded search limitation and do not create candidate-level negative assertions.
