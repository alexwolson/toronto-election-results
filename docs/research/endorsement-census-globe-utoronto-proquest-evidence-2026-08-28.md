# Globe and Mail Toronto municipal endorsements: U of T / ProQuest evidence pass

**Research date/cutoff:** 2026-08-28
**Authenticated source:** ProQuest Global Newsstream Collection, accessed through the University of Toronto Libraries
**Endorser under review:** The Globe and Mail Editorial Board (`globe_and_mail_editorial_board`)
**Scope:** Toronto mayoral and City Council general elections and by-elections, 2003–2026. Trustee contests are excluded.
**Change boundary:** Research evidence only. No canonical assertions, panel membership, curation, or release output were changed in this pass.

## Conclusion

The authenticated archive confirms that the Globe Editorial Board made explicit Toronto mayoral choices in at least three elections:

| Event | Choice | Original item | ProQuest evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2003 general | **John Tory** | *Choosing the next mayor of Toronto*, 2003-11-01, Editorial, p. A24 | Document ID `383973430`; full text says that, if required to mark one X, the board would choose Tory |
| 2010 general | **George Smitherman** | *The Globe's endorsement for Mayor of Toronto*, 2010-10-21 | Document ID `2385373871`; full text explicitly makes Smitherman the board's guarded choice |
| 2014 general | **John Tory** | *Globe editorial board endorsement: John Tory is Toronto's best bet*, 2014-10-17 | Document ID `2383842806`; title and item text explicitly identify Tory |

These are original-source confirmations, not inferences from campaign reporting or third-party summaries. No qualifying Globe board choice was recovered in the bounded authenticated searches for the 2006, 2018, 2022, or 2023 mayoral contests, for council packages, or for council by-elections. Those gaps remain open-world search dispositions, not assertions that the Globe made no endorsement.

## Coverage interpretation

- The Globe has a verified historical Toronto mayoral endorsement practice, but the recovered record is intermittent rather than election-by-election.
- The authenticated evidence does not support constructing a Globe council slate from isolated reporting, candidate claims, or named-columnist views.
- The lack of a recovered 2023 board choice matters because the existing 2026 endorser panel was frozen using a 2023-anchored eligibility rule. Adding the Globe would therefore require an explicit policy change; historical practice alone does not satisfy the current rule.
- A defensible alternative would be to broaden panel eligibility to include organizations with material, verified Toronto editorial-board practice over the historical period, and then include the Globe only in contests where an actual original-source choice is verified. That is a methodology decision, not a data-recovery inference.
