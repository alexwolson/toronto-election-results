# Toronto 2026 endorser-panel evidence matrix

**Research date:** 2026-08-21  
**As-of date for the mayor cohort:** 2026-08-20
**Panel status:** Approved by the project owner on 2026-08-21

## Decision rule and scope

This is an evidence matrix, not a list of people or groups whose views should be
imputed from questionnaires, policy pledges, scorecards, campaign appearances, or
post-election congratulations. An institutional row is eligible only when the *same
exact entity*:

1. publicly selected or endorsed a **certified candidate** in the 2023 Toronto
   mayoral by-election on or before the 8 p.m. close of polls on June 26; and
2. satisfies one structural basis: an established Toronto-wide newspaper editorial
   board with a municipal-endorsement practice; a formal slate spanning at least two
   Toronto mayor/councillor contests in one event; Toronto mayoral endorsements in
   at least two events; or a specifically justified named exception.

Toronto Elections records that there were 102 certified mayoral candidates and that
polls closed at 8 p.m. on June 26. [City by-election report](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/8bb4-Final-for-web-2023-Mayor-ByElection-Report.pdf)
The candidates named in a positive row below—Olivia Chow, Ana Bailão, and Mark
Saunders—were in that certified field. The City Clerk’s official declaration lists
all three by name and their vote totals; the City’s certification release documents
the certified field. [Official declaration of results](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/8eef-Declaration-of-Results-for-the-2023-Toronto-By-Election-for-Mayor-Final.pdf)
[certification release](https://www.toronto.ca/news/toronto-city-clerk-certifies-102-candidates-for-june-26-by-election-for-mayor/)

“Endorsement” is used in its ordinary institutional sense. Progress Toronto is the
one deliberate wording exception: it says it does not “simply endorse” candidates,
but ran a public selection process, selected Chow as its mayoral champion, registered
as a third-party advertiser, and organized to elect her. It is therefore included as
a named exception. Preserve “Progressive Mayoral Champion” as the raw assertion
wording and evidence; under the agreed canonical presentation, the frontend may
still display the semantically broader label “Endorsed by Progress Toronto.”

## Mayor cohort: separate individual panel

This is an individual cohort, not an institutional-endorser gate. It contains living
mayors of amalgamated Toronto as of 2026-08-20: **David Miller, John Tory, and
Olivia Chow**. The City’s chronological list identifies Miller (2004–2010 in the
City’s year convention), Tory (2014–2023), and Chow (2023–present), and identifies
Chow as the fifth mayor since amalgamation. [City historical list](https://www.toronto.ca/explore-enjoy/history-art-culture/mayors-councillors-reeves-chairmen/mayor-reeve-chairman/)
[Chow assumption of office](https://www.toronto.ca/news/mayor-olivia-chow-takes-office-as-mayor-of-toronto/)

The eligibility date is the first assumption of office in amalgamated Toronto—not
the election date, not a prior councillor/trustee role, and not a later re-election.
Living status is frozen at the agreed 2026-08-20 cutoff; a later death does not
retroactively remove a Person or their historical endorsements from this cohort.
