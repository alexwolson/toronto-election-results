## Observed names and identity anchors

## Candidate-controlled career claims

List claims separately from verified election results. Include direct URLs and
access dates. A claim is a research lead, not result evidence.

## Possible prior occurrences

For each lead, record the election date, jurisdiction, office, district, observed
ballot name, direct source URL, and whether the source establishes exact result
facts. Include plausible occurrences later rejected.

## Result evidence

For each proposed occurrence, identify the election authority or other authoritative
result source and record exact votes, total contest votes, vote share, rank, outcome,
and source locator where available.

## Identity bridges

Explain the evidence connecting each occurrence to the 2026 candidate independently
of the name match. Distinguish direct evidence from inference.

## Collision checks

Check same-name people, simultaneous candidacies, incompatible geography, age,
career, biography, and any Person redirects or competing cohort candidates.

## Negative searches and inaccessible sources

Document the databases, jurisdictions, name forms, and query combinations searched,
plus unavailable or non-searchable records.

## Limitations

State anything that materially limits a complete-career conclusion.

## Discovery conclusion

For every proposed occurrence, recommend `confirm`, `hold`, `split`, or `reject` and
briefly explain why. Do not treat absence of a search result as proof of absence.
