### 2018 — one direct support edge; complete slate remains HOLD

The [2018 Campaign Action Plan](https://www.progresstoronto.ca/updates/2018/11/7/campaign-action-plan) says Progress Toronto was supporting progressive candidates and describes a voter-contact operation telling Ward 3 voters why **Amber Morley** was “the best candidate” and asking them to “vote Morley.” This is direct first-party electoral support and is confirmable as an Amber Morley support/endorsement edge. The [2018 City declaration](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/97da-2018clerksofficialdeclarationofresults.pdf) validates Morley as the Ward 3 candidate.

The post-election [Election and Next Steps](https://www.progresstoronto.ca/updates/the-election-and-next-steps) describes the campaigns Progress Toronto worked to elect, but it is post-election and does not supply a complete canonical, pre-election named council slate. Therefore, do not infer the rest of a 2018 slate from the 14 target wards, the post-election winners, or generic references to “progressive candidates.” Those rows remain **HOLD**.

Progress Toronto's own [2023 champion announcement](https://www.progresstoronto.ca/media-release-may10-progressive-mayoral-champions) says its public champion-selection process began with the 2018 cycle, so it should not be transferred backward to the 2016 Ward 2 or 2017 Ward 42 by-elections.

## Elementary Teachers of Toronto (ETT), 2018 — CONFIRMABLE

The exact ETT account, `@ElemTeachersTO`, posted on October 11, 2018: “ETT is so proud to endorse #keesmat4mayor.” The original post is preserved at [ETT's X/Twitter status 1050461558801920000](https://twitter.com/ElemTeachersTO/status/1050461558801920000). The X page itself is currently unavailable to the text retriever, but the exact account, date, URL, and quotation are preserved in the contemporary [2018 Toronto mayoral election endorsement reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Toronto_mayoral_election) (reference 51). This is a direct-account attribution, not an inference from an affiliate or a later congratulations post.

The [City's 2018 declaration](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/97da-2018clerksofficialdeclarationofresults.pdf) validates Jennifer Keesmaat as a candidate in the Toronto mayoral contest. Record ETT → Jennifer Keesmaat, 2018 mayor, as **CONFIRMABLE**.

Do not use the separate [Toronto Occasional Teachers' Local 2018 meeting minutes](https://etfo-torots.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/2018-Oct-16-General-Meeting-Minutes.pdf) to negate this edge. That document concerns a different exact entity, and its failed motion does not speak for ETT.

No qualifying ETT endorsement was recovered for 2016 Ward 2, 2017 Ward 42, 2024 Ward 15, or the current 2026 contests; those cells remain **HOLD**.

## Exclusions and evidence limits

- Exact entity matching is mandatory. ATU Canada, another ATU local, a CUPE local, a school-board body, Toronto Occasional Teachers' Local, and Labour Council are not interchangeable with ATU Local 113, CUPE Ontario, ETT, or another approved institution.
- Anti-candidate campaigns, pledges, issue advocacy, praise, and post-election congratulations are not positive endorsements. They may corroborate a slate only where paired with a direct endorsement announcement.
- The Progress Toronto 2022 primary page is recovered, but its linked nine-name page is currently unavailable. The nine names are therefore supported by primary campaign context plus a contemporaneous reputable direct attribution, with City official-result validation.
- The ATU Local 113 October 6, 2022 slate is an image-only primary source in the current page rendering. Preserve the image in the final evidence package; the ten-name reconstruction is cross-checked against follow-up posts and official City results.
- The report does not infer non-endorsement from silence. Rows marked **HOLD** remain unresolved rather than negative.
