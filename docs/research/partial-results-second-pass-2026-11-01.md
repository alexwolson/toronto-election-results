# Second pass on partial and doubtful results (2026-11-01)

This pass revisited the 1994 French-language trustee Contests, the 1994 Toronto Ward 3 race, the
1994 Metro Don Parkway figure and the 2026 electorate. Two partial Contests were left alone because
no further source is likely to be online: North York Ward 1 in 1995 (see
`by-elections-1995-2011-2026-10-31.md`) and MonAvenir Metro 2 in 1997. ProQuest links are to the
Toronto Star on ProQuest (University of Toronto access) unless noted.

## 1994 French-language areas

Three areas of the Metro Toronto French Language School Council and two of the French-language
section of the Metropolitan Separate School Board were `partial`, because the Star's 16 November
1994 figures ([page A9](https://www.proquest.com/docview/1355150358)) do not reconcile with the
City's archived 1994 page (`data/reference/municipal_archive/results_1994.pdf`). The archive lists
these races under each former municipality, and the earlier pass read each listing as that
municipality's subtotal.

The Scarborough–East York figures show that reading is wrong for the municipality that ran the
area. For all six candidates in the two Scarborough–East York races, the Star's figure is exactly
the archive's Scarborough figure less its East York figure:

| Race | Candidate | Archive, Scarborough | Archive, East York | Star |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| French council | Mohammed Brihmi | 291 | 56 | 235 |
| French council | Jean-Marc Couffin | 201 | 42 | 159 |
| French council | Veronique Perez-McCall | 122 | 38 | 84 |
| French council | Najib Redouane | 86 | 24 | 62 |
| Separate section | Andre Duclos | 376 | 68 | 308 |
| Separate section | Jean Malavoy | 214 | 39 | 175 |

The Scarborough clerk ran the area, so the Scarborough listing is the area's total and the East
York listing is East York's share. The Star printed only Scarborough's polls. Both races now use
the archive's Scarborough figures and are `complete`. The winners do not change.

The separate section's Toronto & York area follows the same pattern less exactly. The archive's
Toronto listing gives Charles Arsenault 659 and Claude-Reno D'Aigle 347, and its York listing 55
and 44. Read as the area total, the Toronto listing leaves 604 and 303 for Toronto's polls, and the
Star's 592 and 302 are 98 and 99.7 per cent of that. Read as Toronto's share alone, the Star's
figures are 90 and 87 per cent of it. The Star's other Toronto figures that night were 93 to 96
per cent of the archive's, because some Toronto polls had not reported. Neither reading matches
exactly, and with the Scarborough evidence the area-total reading is used: 659 and 347, `complete`.

The French council's North York area is North York alone. The Star's seven figures are almost
exactly double the archive's (447 against 225 for Simone Abouchar, 326 against 163 for Alain
Masson, and so on), which looks like a double count. The archive's figures are used and the
Contest is `complete`. The winners do not change.

### Etobicoke–York

The French council's Etobicoke–York seat cannot be settled. The archive's Etobicoke page has no
school-board results. Its York page gives York's polls: Gérard Lévesque 29, Sylviane De
Roquebrune 24, Lise Dubois 13, with Lévesque marked by the asterisk the York page puts on each
race's leader. The Star printed Dubois 89, Lévesque 85 and De Roquebrune 84 and marked Dubois
elected, and the Globe and Mail's list of winners of 15 November 1994 names Dubois
([385130288](https://www.proquest.com/docview/385130288)).

If the Star printed only Etobicoke's polls here, as it did in the other two shared areas, the area
totals are Lévesque 114, De Roquebrune 108 and Dubois 102. The Globe's list was printed the
morning after the vote, before any official declaration, so it adds no independent count. The
Star's preview of 10 November
1994 describes the three candidates ([437131543](https://www.proquest.com/docview/437131543)), and
no later story found names the trustee who sat for the area. The Contest stays `partial` with the
Star's figures, and every row's `elected` is null (`marked_elected=unknown` in the reference
table). The Etobicoke clerk's return at the City of Toronto Archives, or the council's minutes,
would settle it.

## 1994 Toronto Ward 3

The archive gives the election-night count, Mario Silva 2,976 and Tony O'Donohue 2,961. The Globe
and Mail's report of 16 December 1995
([384862991](https://www.proquest.com/docview/384862991), page A4) gives every later count. The
clerk's recount put O'Donohue ahead, 2,942 to 2,933. Chief Judge Sidney Linden's judicial recount
also put him ahead, 2,957 to 2,951. Justice Blenus Wright's second judicial recount on 5 June 1995
counted 40 ballots that Linden had rejected for stray marks and gave Silva the seat, 2,979 to
2,969. The Court of Appeal upheld that count in December 1995, and O'Donohue did not appeal
further. Silva held the seat throughout
([Star, 26 November 1994](https://www.proquest.com/docview/437129429);
[Star, 25 February 1995](https://www.proquest.com/docview/437184252)).

Silva's and O'Donohue's rows now carry 2,979 and 2,969
(`data/reference/municipal_archive_amendments.csv`). No report gives the recount figures for
Fernando Dias Costa and Bob Allisat, so they keep the archive's 344 and 247. A Contest has one
source, so the published Contest keeps the archive as its authority and adds the Globe's report
to its resource and detail. Wikipedia's articles on Silva and O'Donohue say the Court of Appeal
ruled that Silva won by 15 votes, citing a Globe story of 15 December 1995; the Globe's report of
the next day gives 10.

## 1994 Metro Don Parkway

The archive gives Darlene Scott 4,192. The Star printed 4,912 twice, on the morning after the vote
with all 64 polls in ([page E6, 15 November 1994](https://www.proquest.com/docview/1355146363)) and
in its final results the next day ([437139802](https://www.proquest.com/docview/437139802)). Its
percentages (Gordon Chong 39, Scott 28, Simon Lagopoulos 23, Paul Azzarello 6, Ed Ball 4) fit
4,912 and not 4,192, under which Chong would have 41. That shows only that the Star's figure was
the one it used throughout. The Globe printed the winners' names without votes, and no other
source was found. The archive's figure stays, as it does wherever the archive reports a Contest
completely. Either figure leaves Scott second.

## 2026 electorate

The City has not yet published voter statistics for 2026. Its Open Data voter-statistics dataset
holds files up to 2022, and the 2022 file appeared in February 2023. Eligible electors and ballots
cast stay null for 2026. The City's official poll-by-poll results for 2026, posted on 29 October,
give the same mayoral and council totals as the Clerk's declaration the release uses.

## Name capitalisation

The rows that now cite the archive keep the Star's spelling of their names, and archive names are
title-cased, which would have turned Perez-McCall into Perez-Mccall. Title-casing now leaves alone
the capitals a name already has. That also corrects five 1997 names that had lost their inner
capitals: Christopher R. MacDonald, Patrick McCool, Pam McConnell, Michael McDonald and Caroline
Morgan DiGiovanni.
